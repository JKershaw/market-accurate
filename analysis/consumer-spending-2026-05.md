# Executive Summary

This analysis examines whether US consumer spending is on track for the "soft landing" embedded in 2026 retail valuations, or whether the bifurcation between high-income and low-income households has reached the point where aggregate spending data masks a structural problem.

**Central Finding:** Aggregate spending continues to grow nominally, but the composition is unhealthy. Roughly **50% of nominal Personal Consumption Expenditure (PCE) growth in 2025 came from the top 10% of income earners** (Mark Zandi/Moody's analysis), excess pandemic savings have been depleted across the bottom 80%, and credit-card delinquency rates are above pre-pandemic levels. Retail valuations look broadly reasonable, but discount/value retailers are pricing in continued share gains that depend on a recession that the labor market refuses to deliver.

**Key Metrics (April 2026):**

| Metric | Value | Trend |
|--------|-------|-------|
| US retail sales YoY (nominal) | +3.5–4.0% | Slowing from 2024 |
| Real PCE growth YoY | ~2.0% | Below 10-year average |
| Top-10% share of consumption growth | ~50% | Unprecedented |
| Credit-card 30+ day delinquency | ~3.4% | Above pre-COVID |
| Personal savings rate | ~3.8% | Near multi-decade low |
| Excess pandemic savings remaining | ~$0 (bottom 80%) | Depleted |
| Discount-retail SSS (Dollar Tree, Five Below) | Mixed | Trade-down evident |
| Premium-retail SSS (LVMH, Williams-Sonoma) | Resilient | Top-cohort strength |

**Assessment:** Moderate confidence that 2026 features stable aggregate spending with worsening *distribution* of spending. The valuation story differs by tier: discount retail looks priced for a recession that may not arrive, while premium retail depends on equity-market strength holding into 2027.

# The Bifurcation Thesis

## Definition

> The **K-shaped consumption thesis** holds that aggregate consumption growth in 2025–2026 has been driven disproportionately by the top quintile (and especially the top decile) of US households, whose spending is sustained by asset-price appreciation rather than wage growth, while the bottom half has shifted from "post-COVID stimulus" through "excess-savings drawdown" into "credit-funded consumption."

This is not a recession thesis — it is a *fragility* thesis. The macro print can stay stable while the underlying distribution becomes increasingly dependent on the stock market.

## Evidence

### Top-decile dominance

| Source | Finding |
|--------|---------|
| Moody's Analytics (Zandi, 2024–25) | Top 10% accounts for ~50% of consumer spending and ~50% of recent spending growth |
| Federal Reserve Distributional Financial Accounts | Top-10% net worth at record share |
| Visa Spending Momentum Index (decile breakdown) | Top-decile momentum diverging from middle/bottom |

### Bottom-quintile stress signals

| Indicator | Value | Concern Level |
|-----------|-------|---------------|
| Credit-card 30+ day delinquency | ~3.4% | Above 2019 baseline (~2.6%) |
| Auto loan 60+ day delinquency (subprime) | Near cycle highs | Elevated |
| BNPL utilization (Affirm, Klarna) | Rising | Stress proxy |
| Average new auto loan term | ≥70 months | Affordability stress |
| Multifamily rent delinquency (low-tier) | Elevated | Stress proxy |

### Trade-down patterns

Companies reporting explicit trade-down behavior in 2025–2026 earnings:

| Company | Signal |
|---------|--------|
| Walmart | Income mix shift toward $100K+ households |
| Costco | Membership renewals strong; basket size up |
| Dollar Tree | Mixed — some categories up, others under pressure |
| Five Below | Margin compression, traffic moderation |
| McDonald's | "Value menu" emphasis after price-elasticity surprises |
| Target | Discretionary weakness persistent |

# Macro Context

## Real Spending Trajectory

| Year | Real PCE Growth | Real Disposable Income |
|------|----------------|------------------------|
| 2019 | +2.0% | +3.0% |
| 2020 | -2.5% | +6.2% (transfer-driven) |
| 2021 | +8.4% | +1.9% |
| 2022 | +3.0% | -6.0% (real) |
| 2023 | +2.5% | +5.4% |
| 2024 | +2.8% | +3.0% |
| 2025 | ~2.1% | ~1.8% |
| 2026 (YTD) | ~2.0% | ~1.5% |

**Sources:** Bureau of Economic Analysis (BEA) PCE/PI release. 2026 figures are first-quarter approximations subject to revision.

The 2025–2026 pattern is unusual: real income growth is decelerating while real spending continues, financed by savings-rate compression and credit. This is the classic late-cycle consumption pattern observed in 1999–2000 and 2006–2007.

## Savings Rate Compression

| Period | Personal Savings Rate |
|--------|----------------------|
| 2015–2019 average | ~7.5% |
| 2020 peak (April) | 33.7% |
| 2022 trough | 3.0% |
| 2024 average | ~4.5% |
| 2025 average | ~4.0% |
| Q1 2026 | ~3.8% |

A savings rate near 4% is roughly half the pre-pandemic norm. With excess savings substantially depleted for the bottom 80%, future spending growth must come from wages, credit, or asset-price-driven consumption from the top decile.

# Sector-Level Reads

## Discount & Value Retail

| Company | TTM EPS Growth | Forward P/E | Comp Sales Trend |
|---------|---------------|-------------|------------------|
| Walmart | Positive | ~30x | +3–4% comp |
| Costco | Positive | ~50x | +5–6% comp |
| Dollar Tree | Volatile | ~18x | Flat-to-down |
| Dollar General | Pressured | ~16x | Negative comp |
| TJX | Positive | ~27x | +3–4% comp |
| Five Below | Negative | ~22x | Margin compression |

**Read:** The "everything below $5" cohort is bifurcating *within* discount. Walmart and Costco continue to gain top-decile share (treasure-hunt + bulk-value); pure dollar-store concepts dependent on the bottom-quintile customer are struggling. Forward multiples don't fully reflect this — Dollar General's 16x looks cheap until you realize comps may not turn until late 2026.

## Premium & Aspirational Retail

| Company | TTM EPS Growth | Forward P/E | Notes |
|---------|---------------|-------------|-------|
| LVMH | Modest | ~22x | China softness, US resilient |
| Hermès | Strong | ~50x | Top-decile demand persistent |
| Williams-Sonoma | Modest | ~17x | Housing-tied weakness |
| RH (Restoration Hardware) | Negative | ~30x | Levered to housing turnover |
| Lululemon | Decelerating | ~20x | Athleisure penetration peaked |

**Read:** Top-decile spending sustains the high-end so long as US equities hold. The risk is a 15–20% S&P drawdown — which would compress the wealth effect that supports this cohort's consumption. The 2022 drawdown showed the lag is short (1–2 quarters).

## Restaurants & Services Spending

| Segment | Trend |
|---------|-------|
| Quick-service (McDonald's, Wendy's) | Traffic soft, value menus expanding |
| Fast-casual (Chipotle, Sweetgreen) | Premium pricing tested |
| Full-service casual | Weak — Olive Garden, Applebee's |
| Premium experiential (Disney, cruise) | Resilient via top-decile |

The "experience over goods" rotation that defined 2022–2024 has matured. Services-sector PCE growth is decelerating from the post-pandemic catch-up.

# Credit-Cycle Position

| Indicator | 2019 | 2022 | 2024 | 2026 (Q1) |
|-----------|------|------|------|-----------|
| Credit card balances ($T) | $0.93 | $0.99 | $1.17 | ~$1.25 |
| Credit card 30+ DPD | 2.6% | 1.9% | 3.2% | ~3.4% |
| Auto loan 90+ DPD | 4.2% | 3.7% | 4.6% | ~4.7% |
| Mortgage 90+ DPD | 1.0% | 0.5% | 0.6% | ~0.7% |
| Personal bankruptcy filings | Stable | Low | Rising | Rising |

**Mortgage discipline** is the saving grace: 90+ DPD remains low, anchored by 2020–2021 fixed-rate refinances. Credit card and subprime auto are showing late-cycle stress without reaching crisis levels.

The Fed's January 2026 rate-cut path (assumed: 75–100 bps lower by year-end) will help marginal-borrower stress on a 6–12 month lag. This is a tailwind for the bottom-quintile consumer's *interest burden* but does not restore lost real-wage purchasing power.

## Connection to Crypto & Equity Cycles

Daisy-chain dependencies of this kind are historically how recessions transmit faster than expected.

# Predictions

## CS-001: Aggregate spending continues
**Claim:** Real PCE growth for full-year 2026 will be between 1.5% and 2.5% (BEA annual estimate).
**Rationale:** Top-decile resilience plus rate-cut tailwind, balanced against savings-rate compression.
**Confidence:** Moderate (60%)
**Verification date:** February 2027 (BEA annual revision)

## CS-002: Distribution worsens
**Claim:** A major data provider (Moody's, BEA, BlackRock, JPMorgan) will publish 2026 analysis showing the top 10% of households accounted for ≥45% of *real* PCE growth in 2026.
**Rationale:** Continuation of the 2024–2025 pattern absent a wage-growth shock.
**Threshold:** Published estimate ≥45% for 2026 real PCE growth attribution.
**Confidence:** Moderate (60%)
**Verification date:** June 30, 2027

## CS-003: Discount retail divergence
**Claim:** In FY2026 reporting, Walmart's same-store sales growth will exceed Dollar General's by ≥4 percentage points.
**Rationale:** Income-mix shift continues; treasure-hunt format outperforms pure value.
**Threshold:** Reported FY2026 US comp SSS spread ≥4pp.
**Confidence:** Moderate (65%)
**Verification date:** March 2027

## CS-004: Credit stress without crisis
**Claim:** Credit card 30+ day delinquency rate will stay between 3.0% and 4.5% throughout 2026 (no crisis spike, no normalization).
**Rationale:** Bottom-quintile stress persists; rate cuts moderate but don't reverse.
**Threshold:** Quarterly Federal Reserve G.19 release shows monthly delinquency in [3.0%, 4.5%] for all months Jan–Dec 2026.
**Confidence:** Moderate (55%)
**Verification date:** February 2027

## CS-005: Premium-retail equity beta
**Claim:** If the S&P 500 experiences a peak-to-trough drawdown of ≥15% during 2026, then within two quarters Hermès *or* RH will report negative same-store sales (US/Americas region, YoY).
**Rationale:** Top-decile consumption follows wealth effect with short lag; observable in 2022 analogue.
**Threshold:** Conditional — only resolves if the S&P drawdown trigger occurs.
**Confidence:** Moderate (60%, conditional)
**Verification date:** December 31, 2026

# Methodology

## Data Sources

| Source | Usage |
|--------|-------|
| BEA Personal Income & Outlays | Real PCE, savings rate |
| Census Retail Sales (MARTS) | Nominal retail spending |
| Federal Reserve G.19 | Consumer credit |
| New York Fed Household Debt | Delinquency tracking |
| Moody's Analytics (Zandi) | Distributional spending |
| Visa Spending Momentum Index | Categorical spending |
| Company SEC filings | SSS, mix, commentary |

## Limitations

- Distributional spending data lags by 1–2 quarters
- "Top decile share of growth" is sensitive to methodology
- BEA revisions can change the narrative substantially
- Credit data does not capture BNPL exposure cleanly
